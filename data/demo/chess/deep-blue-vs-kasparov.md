# Deep Blue versus Kasparov

IBM built the chess computer Deep Blue to challenge world champion Garry Kasparov. The first match took place in Philadelphia in February 1996. Kasparov won it 4 to 2, although Deep Blue won the first game, the first time a computer beat a reigning world champion under classical time controls.

The rematch was held in New York in May 1997 against an upgraded Deep Blue. This time the machine won 3.5 to 2.5. The decisive moment was Game 6, in which Kasparov played the Caro-Kann Defence and lost in only 19 moves.

Deep Blue searched about 200 million positions per second using custom chips. Kasparov accused IBM of unfair assistance and demanded a rematch that never happened. The matches became a landmark in the history of artificial intelligence.
