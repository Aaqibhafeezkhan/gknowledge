# Endgame Principles

In the endgame the king becomes an active piece. Centralise it early and use it to attack pawns and support passed pawns. Opposition, where the kings face each other with one square between them, often decides king and pawn endings.

Two rook endgame positions are essential knowledge. The Lucena position is a winning technique for the side with the pawn on the seventh rank, known as building a bridge. The Philidor position is a defensive setup that holds a draw by keeping the rook on the sixth rank.

The Tarrasch rule says to place rooks behind passed pawns, whether they are your own or your opponent's. Triangulation is a manoeuvre where a king loses a tempo to pass the move to the opponent. Practising these ideas matters more than memorising openings for improving players.
