# Ruy Lopez

The Ruy Lopez is a chess opening that begins 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5. It is named after Ruy López de Segura, a Spanish priest who analysed it in a book published in 1561. It is also called the Spanish Game.

White pins the knight on c6 to pressure the defender of the e5 pawn. The main line continues 3...a6, the Morphy Defence, and then 4.Ba4 Nf6 5.O-O Be7. The Closed Ruy Lopez leads to slow manoeuvring battles in the centre.

The Berlin Defence, 3...Nf6, became famous when Vladimir Kramnik used it against Garry Kasparov in the 2000 World Championship match in London. Its endgames earned it the nickname "the Berlin Wall". The Marshall Attack is another sharp line in which Black sacrifices a pawn for initiative.
